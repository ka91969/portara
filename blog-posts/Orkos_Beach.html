Orkos Beach: Naxos's Unspoiled Gem Between Plaka and Mikri Vigla

A kilometre of thick, golden sand, broken into a string of small private coves by sharp outcrops of rust-red rock, framed by cedar trees and left entirely undeveloped. Orkos is routinely described by those who know the island well as the single most beautiful beach on Naxos — not for its size, but for a combination of colour, texture and quiet that its larger neighbours simply cannot offer.

ORKOS BEACH: NAXOS'S UNSPOILED GEM BETWEEN PLAKA AND MIKRI VIGLA

 

Orkos lies on Naxos's western coast, roughly eleven kilometres from Naxos Town, occupying the short stretch of shoreline between Plaka to the north and Mikri Vigla to the south. It is, by most measures, one of the smaller named beaches on this side of the island, running for approximately one kilometre in total — yet it is routinely singled out by those familiar with Naxos's full coastline as its most visually striking beach, prized for a distinctive combination of thick golden sand, sheltered rocky coves and a backdrop of rust-red hillside found nowhere else on the island in quite the same concentration.

Two Beaches in One

Orkos divides naturally into two quite different sections. The first, immediately south of Plaka, is a continuous run of roughly 700 metres of sandy shoreline, lined along its full length by trees that provide genuine shade through the hottest hours of the day. The second section, continuing toward Mikri Vigla, consists of a chain of small coves, most no more than 100 to 200 metres long, separated from one another by steep, sharp rocks that plunge directly into the sea. Each of these coves functions, in effect, as its own private beach, sheltered on both sides by rock and filled with the same thick, pale sand found along the beach's northern stretch — a landscape locally compared to nothing else on Naxos.

The sand itself is worth noting specifically: coarser and thicker than the fine sand found at many of Naxos's more developed beaches, it has a practical advantage prized by regular visitors, since it does not cling to skin, hair or clothing the way finer sand often does, and can be lain on directly without a towel or mat.

Red Hills and Cedar Trees

What most distinguishes Orkos visually is the landscape immediately behind it. Low, reddish hills rise directly from the back of the beach, their colour standing in sharp contrast against both the golden sand and the turquoise sea in front of them, while cedar trees grow across the slopes and along stretches of the shoreline itself, framing the coves in green. As the hill of Orkos meets the sea, the view opening out between the cedars, across the coves and out toward the water, is frequently cited as among the most striking coastal panoramas anywhere on Naxos — a landscape shaped as much by geology and colour as by the sea itself.

A Favourite Among Windsurfers

Like several of the beaches along this stretch of Naxos's southwestern coast, Orkos benefits from consistent, favourable wind conditions, and is considered one of the best-known windsurfing beaches on the island. Its relative remoteness and lack of built infrastructure mean it draws a smaller crowd than the more established windsurfing centre at neighbouring Mikri Vigla, giving experienced windsurfers a quieter stretch of water to work with, though the same rocky coves that make the beach so distinctive are not suited to launching kites.

Deliberately Undeveloped

Orkos remains, by design or simply by circumstance, one of the least developed named beaches on this part of the coast. No sunbeds or umbrellas are available for rent, and there are no beach bars or built facilities directly on the sand; visitors intending to spend a full day here are expected to bring their own shade, seating and refreshments. The nearest taverna lies roughly a kilometre away, and the overall absence of infrastructure is, for most who seek the beach out deliberately, precisely the point — a rare stretch of coastline left in something close to its natural state, despite sitting within easy reach of two of the island's most popular beaches.

The approach road itself reinforces that sense of remoteness. The route to Orkos passes through genuinely rural countryside, past potato fields still worked by local farmers and, not infrequently, grazing donkeys along the roadside — a landscape that has changed little despite the tourism development along the coast just beyond it.

Getting There

The most direct route to Orkos follows the relatively recently constructed asphalt road that runs alongside Naxos's airport runway, connecting Naxos Town directly with the island's southwestern beaches. The beach lies close to both Plaka and Mikri Vigla, and can also be reached on foot along the sand from either neighbouring beach for visitors already based nearby. A car remains the most practical way to reach Orkos directly, given the absence of any dedicated public transport stop at the beach itself.

Nearby: Amenities Close By, Without Being Underfoot

Orkos's lack of development is offset by its position: Mikri Vigla, immediately to the south, offers a full range of tavernas, beach bars and accommodation, along with its own well-established windsurfing and kitesurfing scene, all within an easy walk along the coast. Plaka, to the north, adds a further four kilometres of beach, its own tavernas, and its well-known Three Brothers taverna, similarly reachable on foot. Orkos therefore functions less as an isolated beach in its own right and more as a quiet, scenic interlude between two of Naxos's most popular stretches of coast — close enough to their amenities to never feel truly cut off, remote enough to remain, in the words of more than one longtime visitor, the true hidden gem of Naxos's southwestern shore.

 

Orkos rewards precisely the kind of visitor most Naxos beaches cannot: someone willing to arrive with their own towel, walk a little further than the car park, and trade sunbeds and beach bars for red rock, cedar shade and a string of coves that feel, more often than not, entirely private. Few stretches of coast this short pack in quite so much of what makes this island's landscape distinctive.
