Galini, Naxos: A Farming Village in the Eggares Valley

Galini lies on the western side of the Eggares valley, about 5 to 8 kilometres north of Naxos Town on the road towards Apollonas, depending on the source. It is a small farming village of roughly 200 to 300 residents, set above orchards, olive groves and vineyards. It is the nearest of the island’s inland villages to the town, and has a fortified 17th-century monastery, the Ypsilotera, within walking distance.

GALINI, NAXOS: A FARMING VILLAGE IN THE EGGARES VALLEY

 

Galini is one of the closest inland villages to Naxos Town and one of the few small villages on the island that stays active through the year. Its name is the Greek word for “calm” or “peaceful”, and local sources use the word to describe the village, particularly in the evenings. It is built above a fertile valley and shares that valley with Eggares, its neighbour. Its main historical landmark is a fortified monastery that served as a base for local resistance to the Latin nobility in the 17th century.

Location and Setting

Galini is built amphitheatrically on the western side of the Eggares valley, on the slopes of a hill, and overlooks orchards, vineyards and olive groves. Distance from Naxos Town varies by source: 5 kilometres in some, 6 in others, 8 in one. A driving time of about ten minutes is given by a local hotel. It lies on the main road towards Apollonas, and the local road continues to Eggares and down to the coast.

Population figures also differ. The 2021 census is reported to have recorded 235 permanent residents, while guides quote “some 300”, “about 200” or “less than 200”; the differences reflect different counting dates and methods. Local sources note a number of young families, which keeps the village active outside summer. The valley’s vegetable plots and orchards are protected from wind by traditional reed windbreaks, and a tourism source records that the writer Nikos Kazantzakis described the valley as paradisiacal.

The Monastery of Ypsilotera

Roughly 500 metres from the village stands the fortified Monastery of Panagia Ypsilotera (also written Ypsili or Ipsilotera). It is a walled complex whose tower, church and surrounding buildings form what several guides describe as the strongest fortress on the island. Sources give the foundation date variously as 1600, as 1660 and as the early 17th century. They agree on the founder: Iakovos Kokkos, an Orthodox nobleman who opposed Frankish and Latin domination in the Cyclades. It is described as a gathering point for local uprisings against the Latin nobles, which is why the complex is sometimes called a tower of defiance.

Distinctive features include a semicircular tower and, inside the walls, a post-Byzantine church with a notable templon (altar screen). Visitor access is described inconsistently: one regional tourism site says the monastery is open to visitors, while another source states that the tower is not open to the public. The monastery should therefore be treated as primarily an exterior visit unless access is confirmed locally. About 100 metres outside Galini is the church of Panagia Attaliotissa, dedicated to the Dormition of the Virgin.

Farming, Food and Village Life

At its core Galini is a farming village, and the valley produces vegetables, fruit, olives and wine. Several guides mention a small number of traditional tavernas, which are known for local meat, vegetables and wine, as well as a traditional cafe, a mini-market and a small hotel. Services are limited but sufficient for a village of this size, and larger supermarkets are found in Naxos Town. The adjacent village of Eggares has an olive press museum, housed in a renovated building whose original structure dates to the mid-19th century; it is one of several former olive-press buildings that were common across the island. Opening hours are not formally published, so they should be checked locally.

Amitis Beach

The nearest beach is Amitis, about 1.5 kilometres from the village by a road that starts in Galini, with one source giving 9 kilometres from Naxos Town via Galini and 11 kilometres via Eggares. It is described as a quiet, sandy beach with clear water, uncrowded even in high season, but with no facilities. Conditions vary with the wind, since the sea is reported to be rough in northerly winds and calm in southerly ones. Visitors should bring water and supplies.

Visiting Galini

Galini is reached by car or taxi from Naxos Town in a short drive, and the road to Apollonas passes it. Public bus services are seasonal, and routes and timetables for the Galini and Eggares area should be checked at the terminal in Naxos Town. The village is small enough to explore on foot in under an hour, and can be combined with the Ypsilotera monastery, Eggares and Amitis beach in a half-day. For the historic centre of Chora, see the guide to the Kastro of Naxos; for the inland villages further south and east, see the Halki guide. Churches may be locked outside service times, and modest dress is expected inside.

Quick Answers

How far is Galini from Naxos Town? Sources give between 5 and 8 kilometres, a drive of roughly ten minutes.

What is Galini known for? It is known for its position in the Eggares valley, the fortified Ypsilotera monastery and its quiet atmosphere.

What does Galini mean? It is the Greek word for calm or peaceful.

Is there a beach near Galini? Amitis beach is about 1.5 kilometres from the village and is described as quiet and unorganised.

 

Galini and its valley show a side of Naxos that sits close to the capital but remains agricultural: orchards behind reed windbreaks, a fortified monastery that records a 17th-century conflict between local Orthodox nobles and the Latin elite, and a modest beach reached by country road.
