AGII THEODORI: A PEBBLED SHORE BENEATH A WATCHTOWER

 

Agii Theodori lies on Naxos's northern coast, a few kilometres before the road reaches the Tower of Agia, along the same stretch of shoreline that continues north toward Apollonas. It takes its name from a small local chapel dedicated to Agios Theodoros, and it shares the general character of most beaches along this side of the island: long, quiet, entirely unorganised, and reached by very few visitors compared with the busy western coast most tourists never leave.

Named for a Chapel Few Visitors Ever See

As with several beaches covered elsewhere in this series, Agii Theodori's name comes directly from a small church standing near the bay, in this case dedicated to Saint Theodore. The plural form used in both the beach's Greek and English names, Agii or Agioi Theodori, reflects a common pairing in Orthodox tradition of two saints sharing the name Theodore, Theodore Tiro and Theodore Stratelates, often venerated together. The chapel itself remains a modest, unassuming structure, the kind of small local landmark found throughout Naxos's rural coastline rather than a site of any particular historical grandeur.

A Long Beach of Sand and Pebble

The beach itself is long, composed of a mix of sand and pebble rather than the fine golden sand found on much of the western coast, with crystal-clear water and a scattering of distinctive rock formations adding to what most descriptions call an idyllic, if rugged, scene. From the shore, the view extends out across open water toward the islands of Paros and Mykonos, both visible in the distance on a clear day, a genuinely wide-ranging outlook uncommon among Naxos's more sheltered coves.

Isolated, But Not Difficult to Reach

Agii Theodori is consistently described as isolated and unspoiled, a destination suited to visitors specifically seeking solitude rather than company. What sets it apart from several of the other remote beaches covered elsewhere in this series is that its isolation comes without any real difficulty of access: unlike beaches requiring a long walk through dunes or a boat trip to reach at all, Agii Theodori is reachable without particular difficulty by ordinary vehicle, its remoteness a matter of distance and lack of development rather than genuine inaccessibility. That combination, easy to reach yet rarely visited, is relatively unusual on Naxos, where the quietest beaches are more often also the hardest to get to.

No Organisation, Minimal Facilities

The beach is entirely unorganised: no sunbeds or umbrellas are available, there is no water sports provision, and no shade grows along the shore itself. A basic shower and changing area are noted at the site by at least one source, but visitors should otherwise expect nothing beyond the beach itself, and should plan to bring their own food, water and sun protection for a full day here.

The Tower of Agia, a Few Kilometres On

Continuing north along the same coast road from Agii Theodori brings visitors to the Tower of Agia, a fortified structure dating to the 17th century and one of several such towers scattered across Naxos's countryside from the island's long Venetian period, in the same broad tradition as Bazeos Tower, Oskelos Tower and the other fortified rural mansions covered elsewhere in this series. The tower gives its name to the small surrounding settlement of Agia, and its presence on this stretch of coast is a useful landmark for anyone navigating toward Agii Theodori from the north, since the beach lies a short distance before it on the road back toward Amitis and Naxos Town.

Getting There

Agii Theodori is reached via Naxos's northern coast road, the same route serving Amitis and continuing on toward Apollonas, and is noted specifically for being easy to access by any ordinary vehicle without a difficult final stretch or long walk. As with most of the island's northern beaches, a rental car offers considerably more flexibility than public transport, which runs only limited services to this side of the island.

Nearby: The Rest of the Northern Coast

Agii Theodori sits within the same stretch of Naxos's northern shoreline as Amitis, covered elsewhere in this series, a short distance to the south, and the Tower of Agia and the road on to Apollonas, home to the island's largest unfinished ancient kouros, a short distance north. Together, these landmarks make up one of the less-visited corridors of the island, a world away in both pace and infrastructure from the resort beaches of the west coast, but easily incorporated into a longer day exploring Naxos's north.

 

Most of Naxos's quietest beaches ask something of the visitor in return for their solitude, a long walk, a boat booking, an hour of mountain road. Agii Theodori asks for comparatively little: a drive up the northern coast, a willingness to bring your own shade, and an interest in a view that stretches, uninterrupted, all the way to Paros and Mykonos. Few beaches on the island offer that much quiet for that little effort.
