Amitis: A Windswept Bay on the Road North

Between the villages of Galini and Eggares, a wide, open bay on Naxos's northern coast catches whatever wind the day happens to bring, and shows it plainly — calm and glassy when it blows from the south, streaked white with spray when it turns north. Amitis has never tried to be anything other than what the weather makes it.

AMITIS: A WINDSWEPT BAY ON THE ROAD NORTH

 

Amitis lies on Naxos's northern coast, roughly seven to fourteen kilometres from Naxos Town depending on the exact route taken, along the road that eventually continues on toward Apollonas and the great unfinished kouros lying in its quarry at the island's northern tip. Named for the wide bay it occupies rather than for any single settlement, Amitis is shared between the two small villages of Galini and Eggares, each offering its own access route to a different part of the same stretch of sand.

Two Villages, Two Ways In

Amitis can be reached via either of its neighbouring villages, with each route leading to a different section of the same bay. From Eggares, two signposted turns mark the way: the first roughly 1.5 kilometres beyond the village, before a water catchment area, and the second around 2.7 kilometres beyond it, just past an artificial pond, both eventually converging on the same stretch of shore. The alternative route, via Galini, approaches the southern part of the bay instead. Galini's own name, meaning serenity in Greek, sits somewhat at odds with the beach it lends its name to on a windy day, though it captures the mood well enough when the wind drops.

A Long, Open Bay of Pale Sand

The beach itself stretches for nearly 700 metres between the sea and the fertile agricultural land surrounding Eggares, composed of fine, pale sand with stretches of pebble along parts of the shore. A small chapel stands to one side of the bay, a modest, characteristic landmark of the kind found at many of Naxos's quieter beaches. The bay's wide, open orientation, facing directly into the Aegean rather than tucked into any sheltering headland, is the single fact that shapes almost everything else about how the beach actually behaves.

A Beach the Wind Decides

Amitis is protected from southerly winds, and on days when the wind blows from that direction, the bay stays comparatively calm and pleasant for ordinary swimming. When the wind shifts to the north, however, the same open exposure that offers that shelter works against it: the sea turns rough and wavy, occasionally rising to genuinely large waves, and swimming becomes considerably less advisable, with several sources specifically warning of strong currents and undertow, a real hazard for anyone swimming with children on a rougher day. That same wind-driven wave action, unwelcome for ordinary bathers, has made Amitis a genuine draw for windsurfers, who value the bay's open exposure and reliable wind precisely because of the same conditions that keep more casual swimmers away when the weather turns.

Quiet Even in August

Every account of Amitis converges on the same basic description: quiet, uncrowded, and largely untouched even during the busiest weeks of the Naxian summer. It is not a beach that draws crowds even when the island's western resorts are full, and its remoteness from the main tourist circuit, combined with its exposure to the weather, keeps it a destination chosen deliberately by visitors seeking solitude rather than stumbled upon by passing traffic.

Minimal, With One Small Exception

Amitis is not an organised beach: no sunbeds or umbrellas are available to rent, and the overwhelming majority of the shoreline offers no facilities of any kind. The one consistent exception is a small snack stand or beach canteen operating nearby, giving visitors at least a basic option for a cold drink or a light bite without needing to drive back to Galini or Eggares. Beyond that, a shower, changing room and toilet are noted at the site by at least one source, though visitors should not expect anything close to the infrastructure found at Naxos's larger organised beaches.

Getting There

Access to Amitis is via asphalt road for most of the distance from Naxos Town, with a short final stretch of dirt road easily manageable by ordinary car or motorbike. Public transport is limited to a single bus per day during the summer season, making a rental car or motorbike the only realistic option for most visitors rather than a matter of simple convenience.

Nearby: The Road to Apollonas

Amitis sits along the same north-coast road that continues on toward Apollonas, the fishing village home to the largest of Naxos's unfinished ancient kouroi, covered elsewhere in this series. Galini and Eggares, the two villages sharing the bay, offer the nearest accommodation and services for visitors wanting to stay in the immediate area rather than basing themselves back in Naxos Town.

 

Amitis rewards a very specific kind of visitor: one checking the wind direction before deciding whether today is a swimming day or a windsurfing day, rather than assuming the beach will simply behave the same way it did yesterday. Between two quiet villages, on a stretch of coast most visitors drive straight past on their way somewhere else, it remains exactly what nearly every source calls it — a place to relax, when the weather allows it.
