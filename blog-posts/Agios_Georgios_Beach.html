AGIOS GEORGIOS BEACH: NAXOS TOWN'S OWN STRETCH OF SAND

 

Agios Georgios lies immediately south of Naxos Town, so close to the centre of Chora that it barely qualifies as a separate destination at all: the beach begins roughly 300 metres from Plateia Protodikiou, the town's main square, and is reachable entirely on foot from the harbour, the Kastro or any of the town's hotels. That proximity, more than any single quality of the sand or water, is what defines Agios Georgios — it is, by a wide margin, the most visited and most crowded beach on Naxos, the natural choice for anyone unwilling to trade the convenience of staying in town for the quieter sands further along the coast.

A Chapel, and a Harbour Older Than the Kastro

The beach takes its name from a small chapel dedicated to Saint George standing at its northern end, close to where the sand meets the edge of Naxos Town itself. Long before it became a beach in the tourist sense, this stretch of coastline served a more practical function: it operated as a natural harbour for the residents of Chora, in use even during the period when the Venetian dukes of the Duchy of the Archipelago were still constructing their fortifications on the Kastro hill immediately above. That layered history, an ancient anchorage sitting directly beneath a medieval citadel, gives Agios Georgios a genuine sense of continuity with the town it borders, even though no ruins survive on the sand itself. The Portara, the surviving doorway of Naxos's unfinished Temple of Apollo, stands only a couple of kilometres away along the same shoreline, and the Kastro's walls remain visible from much of the beach.

A Curved Bay of Golden Sand

The beach forms a curved bay of soft, golden sand and clear turquoise water running for approximately one kilometre along the town's southern edge, with water calmest toward its northern end, where a small finger of land offers some shelter from the prevailing northerly winds. The seabed shelves gradually across most of the bay, keeping the water shallow for a considerable distance from shore, a characteristic that makes Agios Georgios consistently recommended as one of the safest and most convenient beaches on the island for families travelling with young children. Naxos Town's own beach has been recognised with Blue Flag status, the international award given for water quality, safety and environmental management, reflecting standards maintained despite the beach's heavy daily use.

The Busiest Beach on the Island

Agios Georgios's convenience comes with an obvious trade-off: it is widely regarded as the busiest, most crowded beach on Naxos, drawing large numbers of visitors throughout the day simply by virtue of requiring no real effort to reach. Sunbeds and umbrellas cover much of the sand during the summer season, and cafés and tavernas line the beachfront in considerable density, many of them doubling as lively spots for an evening drink or dinner once the daytime crowds have thinned. Visitors seeking more open space without leaving the beach entirely generally head toward its southern end, where the crowds ease somewhat and the beach's character shifts toward its sporting facilities.

Windsurfing at the Southern End

The southern stretch of Agios Georgios is home to a well-equipped water sports centre and is considered one of the better-known windsurfing spots on Naxos, thanks to a reliable combination of shallow water and consistent wind that also makes it a popular location for lessons. Beginners and more experienced windsurfers alike make use of this section of the beach, kept somewhat apart from the main swimming and sunbathing areas further north.

Views Toward Paros

Like several of Naxos's western-facing beaches, Agios Georgios offers a clear view across the water toward the neighbouring island of Paros, along with reliably good sunsets over the same stretch of sea — a fitting bonus for a beach whose greatest asset is otherwise simply being exactly where visitors already are.

Amenities and Accessibility

Given its location within Naxos Town itself, Agios Georgios benefits from a level of infrastructure few other beaches on the island can match: showers, changing facilities, toilets, lifeguard cover, and a dense cluster of shops, cafés and restaurants immediately behind the sand, all within the same short walk that connects the beach to the rest of Chora. The beach also offers notably good accessibility for visitors with mobility needs, a further point in its favour for travellers prioritising ease of access over seclusion. Parking is available nearby for those arriving by car, though most visitors staying in Naxos Town reach the beach on foot.

Getting There

Agios Georgios requires no real journey at all for anyone staying in or near Naxos Town: it lies within easy walking distance of the harbour, the Kastro and the town's main square, and is often the first beach reached by visitors simply following the coastline south from the port. For those staying further afield, the beach is easily reached by car, taxi or local bus service into the centre of town.

Nearby: The Start of a Much Longer Coastline

Agios Georgios marks the beginning of Naxos's long run of western and southwestern beaches, continuing on toward Agios Prokopios, Agia Anna, Maragas, Plaka and beyond — each covered elsewhere on this blog, each progressively quieter and less developed the further one travels from town. For visitors based in Chora itself, Agios Georgios also sits within easy reach of the Portara, the Kastro and the Archaeological Museum, making it entirely possible to combine a morning of sightseeing with an afternoon on the sand without ever needing to get in a car.

 

Agios Georgios will rarely be anyone's pick for the most beautiful beach on Naxos, and it makes no claim to be; its appeal lies elsewhere, in the sheer convenience of a genuine sandy bay, shallow and safe, sitting close enough to Chora's tavernas and museums that a swim requires no more planning than stepping out of a hotel door. For a first afternoon on the island, or a last one before a ferry home, few beaches on Naxos make more practical sense.
