AGIOS FYKAS: A SMALL, WIND-FREE BAY NEAR APOLLONAS

 

Agios Fykas lies on Naxos's northeastern coast, close to the fishing village of Apollonas and, by some accounts, also associated with the nearby settlement of Komiaki further along the same stretch of shoreline, roughly thirty-five kilometres from Naxos Town. It is a small beach by most measures, and a genuinely little-known one even by the standards of an island with no shortage of quiet corners, but it holds a specific, practical appeal that has kept it in local circulation regardless: a bay sheltered enough to stay calm when the wind is disturbing almost everywhere else.

Sand, Pebbles and Genuinely Wind-Free Water

The beach is generally described as sandy with crystal-clear water, soft and shallow enough to suit young children, though at least one detailed account also describes a scattering of smooth, varied pebbles along parts of the shore, striking enough in shape and colour that visitors have compared arranging them to composing a small mosaic. What is consistently singled out above either the sand or the pebbles, however, is the bay's shelter: Agios Fykas sits protected enough from the prevailing wind to remain calm on days when Naxos's more exposed coastlines are considerably rougher, a genuinely useful quality on an island where the meltemi can disrupt plans at a moment's notice through the summer months.

Swimming, Fishing and a Sunset Dive

Agios Fykas is used locally as much for fishing as for swimming, its calm, clear water suiting both equally well, and it has also been recommended specifically as a spot for a late dive at sunset, when the last light catches the bay with little else around to distract from it. Descriptions of the beach lean toward the enthusiastic, calling it a small paradise largely unknown to the general visitor, the kind of place discovered only by those already exploring this remote corner of the island rather than by anyone consulting a standard beach list.

A Possible Namesake Nearby

Naxos's northeastern coast also holds a separate, similarly named beach referred to as Fykio, historically reached via an 800-metre footpath through the village of Messaria and today accessible by dirt road, turning off shortly before the village itself. Given the shared root of the two names, both drawing on the Greek word for seaweed, it is worth noting that some visitors and sources use Agios Fykas and Fykio somewhat interchangeably, or may be describing beaches close enough together to be easily confused with one another; travellers relying on a specific set of directions from either name are best served confirming the exact route locally rather than assuming both refer to precisely the same cove.

No Facilities, and Very Little Company

Agios Fykas offers no organised infrastructure of any kind: no sunbeds, no umbrellas, no beach bar or taverna directly on the sand. Visitors should treat it as a genuinely undeveloped beach and plan accordingly, bringing shade, food and water for the day. Its remoteness and lack of any real profile beyond local knowledge mean it sees a fraction of the visitors found even at Naxos's other quiet northern and eastern beaches.

Getting There

Reaching Agios Fykas means committing to the full drive up Naxos's eastern side, roughly thirty-five kilometres from Naxos Town via the island's mountainous interior, continuing past Apollonas or toward Komiaki depending on the exact route taken. A rental car is effectively essential, since this stretch of coast sees minimal public transport, and the beach itself is best treated as a deliberate detour on a longer day spent exploring the island's northern tip rather than a stop along a more conventional itinerary.

Nearby: Apollonas and the Northern Tip

Agios Fykas sits within easy reach of Apollonas, the fishing village home to the colossal unfinished Kouros of Apollonas, covered elsewhere in this series, and a natural base for anyone exploring this far corner of the island. The village of Komiaki, along the same stretch of coast, and the mining village of Koronos and its own emery heritage further inland round out a part of Naxos rarely covered in typical visitor itineraries, but genuinely rewarding for anyone who has already spent time on the island's busier western beaches.

 

Most of Naxos's beaches are found by consulting a list. Agios Fykas is more often found by accident, a stop made by visitors already committed to the long drive out to Apollonas who decide, on the way, to see what else this stretch of coast is quietly keeping to itself. What they generally find is a small, calm, wind-sheltered bay that has never needed to announce itself to anyone.
