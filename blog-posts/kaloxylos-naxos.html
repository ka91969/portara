KALOXYLOS (KALOKSILOS), NAXOS: A TRADITIONAL VILLAGE OF THE TRAGEA VALLEY

 

Kaloxylos is one of the smaller villages of the Tragea valley, in central Naxos, immediately next to Halki. It has no single headline monument, but it has a well-preserved village core, two small museums and several nearby Byzantine chapels. Both spellings, Kaloxylos and Kaloksilos, appear in guides and on signs.

Location and Setting

Kaloxylos stands in the Tragea valley, which is the island’s largest olive-growing area, and is surrounded by olive groves, vineyards, and plane and oak trees. It is listed as one of the villages of Tragea alongside Halki, Akadimi, Monitsia (Rachi), Kerami, Metochi and others; for the wider area see the Halki guide. One regional source gives the population as about 140, and describes the residents as working mainly in agriculture and olive-oil production. The village has been listed as a traditional settlement since 1988 according to one source.

A tourism site notes that the village was once known as “To Mikro Parisi” (“the Little Paris”) for its elegance. This is a nickname from local description and has no documented origin. The village is also noted for the rare flowers in its courtyards.

Agia Triada

The church of Agia Triada (Holy Trinity) stands at the entrance to the village and dominates it. Guides mention a carved marble iconostasis and Byzantine icons. One guide adds that an angel on the iconostasis is the work of the sculptor Yannoulis Halepas, one of the best-known Greek sculptors; this attribution comes from a single tourism source and should be confirmed before being repeated. A regional history site records that a tower built in the village by the Venetian Kokkos family was torn down by the villagers, who used its large stones to build the village church; the sources reviewed do not state which church, so it should be read as a local account.

Folklore Museum and Olive Press

Near the church are two small museums. The folklore museum holds exhibits on the history and traditional way of life of Kaloxylos and, in a broader sense, of Naxos; a local guide says it was assembled over about 20 years by Florios Chorianopoulos, who collected items from across the island.

The second building is a restored olive press. Guides report that it displays the tools used for processing olives and a generator that supplied electricity to nearby villages. Kaloxylos once had four pre-industrial olive mills, of which two are said to survive. Opening hours for both museums are not formally published and should be confirmed locally.

Chapels Around the Village

Many small Byzantine chapels are scattered through the surrounding hills, which are described as ideal for walking. Two are most often named: Panagia Damiotissa (also written Damniotissa) and Agios Ioannis Theologos. One source dates the Damiotissa church to the 9th–10th century and notes interesting murals; another describes it as a cruciform domed church with frescoes of the 12th and 13th centuries, with later additions in the 14th. The two descriptions can be reconciled if the building is earlier than most of its surviving paintings, but exact dates are uncertain. Because these are small rural churches, they are often locked outside service times, and access should be arranged locally.

Walking

Kaloxylos is described as the starting point of many walking trails leading to whitewashed chapels and other sights in the valley. A published circular route links Halki, Monitsia, Moni, Kaloxylos and Akadimi (see the Moni guide and the Vourvouria and Akadimi guide). Paths can be rough and unshaded, and signage varies, so water and a map or downloaded route are advisable.

Visiting Kaloxylos

Kaloxylos is reached by car or taxi from Naxos Town through the Tragea valley, a short distance from Halki; public bus services are seasonal, and routes and timetables should be checked at the terminal in Naxos Town. The village can be seen in about an hour and combines easily with Halki, Damarionas (Damarionas guide), Akadimi and Moni. Services in the village are limited, and cafes and tavernas are found in Halki. Because Kaloxylos is a residential village, visitors should keep to the lanes and public spaces, and treat churches as places of worship. Late spring and early autumn are generally the more comfortable seasons for walking.

Quick Answers

Where is Kaloxylos? In the Tragea valley of central Naxos, next to Halki.

What is Kaloxylos known for? It is known for its protected village character, the Agia Triada church, a folklore museum, a restored olive press and nearby Byzantine chapels.

Is Kaloxylos the same as Kaloksilos? Yes. Both are transliterations of the same Greek village name.

How many people live in Kaloxylos? One regional source gives about 140.
