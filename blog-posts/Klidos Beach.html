Klidos: Not One Beach, But a Handful of Them

Between Psili Ammos and Panermos, small rocky headlands break the eastern coastline into a string of separate coves, each with its own sand, its own orientation, and its own quiet. Locally these are known collectively as Klidos — less a single beach than a loose family of them, sharing a name and very little else.

KLIDOS: NOT ONE BEACH, BUT A HANDFUL OF THEM

 

Klidos, sometimes spelled Kleidos, lies on Naxos's remote southeastern coast, occupying the stretch of shoreline between Psili Ammos to the north and Panermos to the south, next to the small settlement that shares its name. Rather than a single continuous stretch of sand, Klidos is best understood as a complex of separate coves: small rocky peninsulas jut out into the sea at intervals along this part of the coast, dividing it into a series of individual beaches, each with its own particular character, orientation and mix of sand, pebble and rock. Together they form one of the quietest, least visited corners of an already quiet stretch of coastline.

A String of Small Coves

The beaches that make up Klidos share a general character, crystal-clear water and a generally grey-beige sand, distinct from the golden tones found at Psili Ammos immediately to the north, but each individual cove within the complex has its own personality. Some are genuinely sandy and shallow, among the shallowest anywhere on Naxos, making them well suited to families on a calm day; others are smaller and more pebbled, better suited to a quick swim or a spot of fishing than to a full afternoon spread out on a towel. Interesting rock formations punctuate the coastline throughout, and the overall landscape is consistently described as wild and rugged rather than manicured, in keeping with the largely undeveloped character of this entire side of the island.

Quiet Enough for Fishing, Not Much Else

Visitor accounts of Klidos consistently emphasise just how deserted the area tends to be, even relative to other beaches on this already sparsely visited coast. The seclusion and calm water along much of the complex has made it a favoured spot for casual fishing as much as for swimming, and several of its smaller coves see so little traffic that a visitor can reasonably expect to have an entire cove to themselves outside the busiest weeks of summer. This is not a beach complex built around organised activity; it exists largely as a quiet backdrop for whoever happens to stop.

Sharing a Landmark with Psili Ammos

Klidos sits close enough to Psili Ammos that the two are sometimes discussed together, and at least one well-known local landmark, the Theonis Villas apartment complex, used elsewhere in this series as the signpost marking the walk down to Psili Ammos, sits close to this stretch of coast as well, reflecting just how tightly clustered the handful of settlements and beaches along this part of the shoreline actually are. Visitors exploring one are, in practice, usually within easy reach of the other.

No Organisation, No Real Facilities

As with nearly every beach along this stretch of Naxos's eastern coast, Klidos offers no sunbeds, umbrellas or beach bars, and visitors should plan to bring their own shade, food and water. The nearest amenities of any real substance are back in Moutsouna to the north or in Apeiranthos inland, both a genuine drive away rather than a short walk, reinforcing that a visit here is best planned as a deliberate stop on a longer day exploring the eastern coast rather than a spontaneous detour.

Getting There

Klidos is reached via the same coastal road serving Psili Ammos and Panermos, asphalt for most of the distance between the individual coves that make up the complex, though the wider route to reach this side of the island, from Naxos Town via Chalki, Filoti and a narrow, winding road beyond Apeiranthos, demands genuine care and takes well over an hour to drive. A car remains the most practical way to reach Klidos, though a motorbike or scooter is equally capable of covering the final stretch of coastal road linking the individual beaches together.

Nearby: The Rest of the Southeastern Coast

Klidos sits at the heart of the same remote coastal stretch covered by several other entries in this series: Psili Ammos and its cedar-backed dunes lie just to the north, while Panermos, with its own beach, sheltered harbour and the Early Cycladic fortified settlement of Korfari ton Amygdalon on the hill above it, lies a short distance south, marking the point where the coastal road finally ends and the boat-only bays around Spedo and Rina's Cave begin. Moutsouna, with its old emery-export harbour, anchors the wider area a little further north.

 

Most beaches ask to be judged as a single place. Klidos resists that framing almost by design: a scattering of small coves, no two quite alike, joined mainly by how little anyone visits them. For travellers who have already worked their way through Naxos's busier western coast and are looking for the point where the map simply runs out of names, Klidos is exactly that kind of unclaimed, unhurried stop.
